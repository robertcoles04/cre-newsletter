Top Stories

Silicon Valley office leasing notches second-highest quarter ever

Silicon Valley recorded its second-highest office leasing quarter on record, a sharp turn for a market hit hard by remote work. The demand points to tenants committing to space again. The Real Deal

Why it matters: Strong leasing lets Valley landlords fill empty space and push rents, which makes refinancing their buildings far easier.

Blackstone's LivCor sells Rampart Village apartments for 33% less than 2018 price

Blackstone's LivCor sold the Rampart Village apartment complex for 33% less than it paid in 2018. The sale marks a sizable loss for one of the market's largest owners. The Real Deal

Why it matters: A marquee seller taking a loss sets a fresh, lower comp that nearby apartment buyers and appraisers will point to.

Johnson revives corporate head tax as $882M budget shortfall looms

Chicago Mayor Johnson is reviving a corporate head tax, a per-employee charge on larger firms, to help close an $882 million budget shortfall. The tax would raise the cost of keeping workers in the city. The Real Deal

Why it matters: A tax tied to headcount raises the cost of a downtown office, which can push tenants to shrink space or leave.

Real Capital, Dunton Commercial buy at Denver Tech Center as office market hits bottom

Real Capital and Dunton Commercial teamed up for a bargain purchase at the Denver Tech Center, buying office space they believe is priced at the market's floor. The duo is betting values have stopped falling. The Real Deal

Why it matters: When opportunistic buyers start calling a bottom, they give struggling Denver office owners a comp and some hope of a floor on prices.

Why it matters: Lenders writing a check this size for ground-up office means they believe top-tier Manhattan space will lease, even as older towers struggle.

Why it matters: Refinancing buys an owner time to hold a property instead of selling into a weak market, keeping the asset off the distressed pile.

Market Watch

Sun Belt

Trepp reports that $3.04 billion across 130 performing floating-rate agency multifamily loans in Texas, or 25.3% of that performing balance, shows a debt service coverage ratio below 1.0x. That means the properties' reported cash flow is not enough to cover their loan payments, even though the loans are still current. Trepp

Why it matters: A coverage ratio under 1.0x is an early warning, so Texas apartment owners with floating-rate debt may need to add cash or sell before loans go bad.

West Coast

A lender is pushing for the sale of a Sun Valley studio owned by Blackstone and Hudson Pacific that now sits completely vacant. The move suggests the lender wants out rather than waiting for a tenant. The Real Deal

Why it matters: When a lender forces a sale of an empty building, it signals they have given up on a quick turnaround and will accept a lower price to exit.

International

Canadian office availability fell to 15.1% as the pipeline of new construction shrinks. Fewer new buildings means existing space gets absorbed instead of competing with brand-new supply. Connect CRE Canada

Why it matters: Falling availability with less new construction gives Canadian office landlords room to hold or raise rents as tenants compete for fewer options.

Term of the Day

Sale-leaseback

A deal where a company sells a property it owns, then signs a lease to keep using it. The seller frees up cash tied in the building while staying in place as a tenant. For example, a retailer might sell its store for $10 million and lease it back for 15 years. The buyer gains a steady rent; the seller gains capital to spend elsewhere.
