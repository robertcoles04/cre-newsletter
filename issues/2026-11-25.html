Top Stories

Ransomware hits a major multifamily debt broker

A ransomware group called Qilin, which has ties to Russia, hacked Meridian Capital Group, one of the country's largest multifamily debt brokerages. Ransomware locks up a company's files until it pays to get them back.

Why it matters: Meridian arranges loans for apartment owners, so a breach could expose borrower financials and slow financing for deals already in its pipeline.

Source: Bisnow: Ransomware hits a major multifamily debt broker

Apartment buyers push for discounts on signed deals

U.S. apartment buyers are asking for 8 percent price cuts on deals they already agreed to, and most sellers are saying no. Higher interest rates and rising cap rates have squeezed buyers since they signed, while looming loan maturities add pressure.

Why it matters: when buyers and sellers cannot agree on price, deals collapse, and the standoff keeps apartment sales volume stuck at low levels.

Source: Bisnow: Apartment buyers push for discounts on signed deals

Debt Markets

The appraisal on downtown Portland's largest hotel portfolio plunged 83 percent, and the investors who lent against it now face steep losses. The two hotels back commercial mortgage-backed securities (CMBS), bonds built from pooled commercial property loans, so the write-down flows straight through to bondholders. The Business Journals Why it matters: when one portfolio can erase this much value, CMBS buyers grow warier of hotel loans, and other hotel owners pay more to borrow.

Trepp reports that early CMBS payoffs and defeasance are the strongest since 2022, while loan liquidations are the most since 2019 and losses are running a little higher. Defeasance is when a borrower swaps in safe securities to retire a loan early. Trepp Why it matters: healthy borrowers are clearing loans early while weak ones are forced out at a loss, a split that shows lenders which corners of the market are still financeable.

AAA-rated bonds are supposed to be the safest layer of a commercial mortgage bond, paid back before everyone else. A new analysis warns that heavy losses on hotel loans could be large enough to wipe out even those top-rated slices inside some CMBS (bonds backed by pooled commercial mortgages). That would break the core promise that senior bondholders get paid first. Propmodo.

Why it matters: If even AAA hotel-bond buyers can lose money, lenders will demand higher rates on new hotel loans, making it costlier for owners to borrow or refinance.

Distress Watch: Trepp found CMBS tied to the five cranberry-growing states ran 9.05 percent delinquent in October, above its 7.87 percent national rate, led by Oregon hotels. Trepp

Market Watch

Sun Belt

Austin's multifamily construction pipeline shrank last quarter, according to new data, as fewer apartment projects break ground in one of the Sun Belt's hottest rental markets. Builders have pulled back after years of heavy supply. The Business Journals Why it matters: less new supply ahead means existing Austin apartment owners face less competition, which can help their rents and occupancy recover.

West Coast

Meta is weighing a sublease of 160,000 square feet at its downtown San Francisco office hub. Subleasing means a tenant rents out space it still holds on its own lease. The Real Deal Why it matters: when a large tenant tries to shed space, it adds to San Francisco's office glut and pushes landlords to cut rents to compete.

International

Dubai office rents are climbing as vacancy has fallen to 3.9 percent, with steady demand from companies setting up in the emirate absorbing available space. The market stands in sharp contrast to softer Western office hubs. Gulf Daily News (Bahrain) Why it matters: tight vacancy and rising rents make Dubai offices attractive to developers and investors hunting for growth outside slower markets.

Term of the Day

Appraisal

A licensed professional's estimate of what a property is worth in today's market, which lenders usually require before they make a loan. If a building appraises for $10 million but still carries a $12 million mortgage, the lender is suddenly under-collateralized, and the bonds backed by that loan lose value.
