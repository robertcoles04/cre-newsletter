Homebuilder confidence sinks to a 14-year low

Builder sentiment fell to its lowest level in 14 years, and price cuts are not pulling buyers back into new homes. High mortgage rates and sticker shock are keeping shoppers on the sidelines.

Top Stories

A landlord allegedly pledged the same equity twice

Investors claim Mike Kohan double-pledged $200 million in equity, including Chicago skyscrapers, to raise money from more than one set of bond buyers. The suit says the same collateral backed competing claims.

Why it matters: lenders rely on clean collateral, so if one owner can pledge a building twice, bond investors may demand tougher checks and higher rates on everyone.

Source: The Real Deal: A landlord allegedly pledged the same equity twice

NYC retail is busy, but storefronts stay empty

Foot traffic and leasing demand in New York are strong, yet many street-level stores sit vacant. Landlords holding out for top rents and slow build-outs are leaving space dark.

Why it matters: empty storefronts cut a building's income even in a hot market, which can lower its value when the owner goes to refinance.

Source: The Real Deal: NYC retail is busy, but storefronts stay empty

Apartment insurance costs outrun rent growth

Insurance premiums are rising faster than revenue for apartment owners across 30 states.

Why it matters: every extra dollar of insurance is a dollar of lost net income, which squeezes owners already fighting flat rents and high loan payments.

Source: GlobeSt.com: Apartment insurance costs outrun rent growth

Real estate fundraising drops 31%

Money raised for real estate funds fell 31 percent as investors steered capital toward residential and industrial deals. Office and niche strategies are drawing far less.

Why it matters: less fund money means fewer buyers for large assets, so sellers may have to cut prices to get deals done.

Source: GlobeSt.com: Real estate fundraising drops 31%

Debt Markets

Nearly a third of maturing apartment loans cannot refinance without fresh equity, with about $6.8 billion in new cash needed to clear loans written at 2021 and 2022's low rates. One example, a 767-unit tower near Wall Street that was 94 percent full, shows how even healthy buildings face the gap. Commercial Observer Why it matters: owners must either write big checks or sell, so expect more forced sales of otherwise sound apartments.

Major banks are taking larger losses as they clear problem commercial real estate loans off their books. Nonaccrual balances, loans that no longer collect interest, are falling as lenders write them down. Bisnow Why it matters: banks cleaning house now free up room to lend again, which could slowly reopen credit for borrowers.

Rising Treasury yields are testing borrowers who hoped falling rates would bail out their loans. Higher yields push up the cost of any new debt they take on. GlobeSt.com Why it matters: borrowers counting on cheaper refinancing may find rates stay high, forcing tougher choices when their loans mature.

AmTrust is set to lose Illinois Center, its Chicago office complex, in a foreclosure auction after defaulting on $285M in debt. A foreclosure auction lets lenders sell a property to recover what they are owed when the borrower stops paying. The Real Deal.

Why it matters: When an owner cannot pay its loan and loses the building, the lender eats the loss, a warning sign that older city office towers are worth far less than their debt.

Distress Watch: Blackstone's $1.3 billion CMBS loan has moved to special servicing, the team that handles troubled loans, a sign distress is reaching even large institutional owners. The Real Deal

Market Watch

Sun Belt

Houston's apartment market is bracing for a wave of foreclosures as investors wait for prices to bottom. Higher rates and thin equity have left many pandemic-era buyers stuck in deals they can no longer carry. Bisnow Why it matters: forced sales set low comps, so patient buyers may scoop up Houston apartments cheaply while current owners take the loss.

West Coast

A loan on a distressed downtown Los Angeles office tower owned by Brookfield sold for $115 million. Buyers of discounted debt often aim to take control of the building itself. The Real Deal Why it matters: selling the loan at a markdown resets the tower's value and signals how far distressed office prices have fallen.

International

Developer Mohamed Alabbar unveiled plans to invest up to $17 billion in Syria. The bet targets rebuilding in a market opening up after years of conflict. Middle East Construction News Why it matters: large reconstruction bets abroad show where some developers see growth while many US deals stay frozen.

Term of the Day

Special servicing

The specialist team that takes over a troubled CMBS loan (a mortgage bundled into bonds and sold to investors) to modify it, extend it, or foreclose. When a borrower stops paying, the loan leaves its normal servicer and lands here. Bondholders gain a dedicated workout team; the borrower loses easy flexibility.
