Top Story

Apartment construction falls to its lowest level since 2012

Multifamily starts, the count of new apartment projects that break ground, dropped to their weakest pace since 2012. Developers are pulling back as high financing costs and softer rents make new projects hard to justify. GlobeSt.com

Why it matters Fewer projects now means less new supply in two to three years, which supports rents for landlords but makes affordability worse for renters.

Top Stories

Tyko places a multibillion-dollar bet on South Florida

Tyko is assembling a large portfolio across South Florida, a multibillion-dollar wager on the region's continued growth. The Real Deal

Why it matters: Concentrated buying by one firm sets price comparisons, called comps, that nearby owners can point to when they sell or refinance.

Hotel sales volume jumps 30% as deal sizes grow

Hotel transaction volume rose 30% in the third quarter, and the average deal got bigger. Buyers are moving back into hospitality. GlobeSt.com

Why it matters: Rising volume tells sellers that buyers and their lenders are comfortable pricing hotels again, which makes it easier to sell or refinance one.

Court upholds Section 8 protections as Hochul vetoes a broker bill

A court upheld rules barring New York landlords from rejecting tenants who pay with Section 8, the federal voucher that subsidizes rent for lower-income renters. Governor Hochul separately vetoed a bill that would have changed who pays broker fees. The Real Deal

Why it matters: Landlords must keep accepting voucher tenants, while the vetoed bill leaves the current broker-fee rules in place for renters and agents.

Historic Loop office above Macy's sells at a steep discount

A historic office property above the Macy's in Chicago's Loop sold at a substantial discount to its earlier value. The sale is one more marked-down office trade in a struggling downtown. The Real Deal

Why it matters: A discounted sale sets a low comp that drags on other Loop office values and makes refinancing those buildings harder.

Debt Markets

A landlord tied to Kelemen handed the keys to several Orange County offices back to its lender after owing more than $100 million on the loans. Giving back the keys means the owner walked away instead of keeping up payments, letting the lender take the buildings. The Real Deal

Why it matters: When owners decide a building is worth less than its loan, lenders eat the loss and nearby office values take another hit.

Namdar kept building in Miami, lining up $340 million in financing for a Brickell mixed-use project. A financing is the loan a developer takes out to fund construction, and a lender willing to write a check this size signals confidence in the submarket. GlobeSt.com

Why it matters: A loan this size tells other developers that lenders still fund new Miami projects, giving them a reference point for their own deals.

Apollo provided a $305 million loan to convert 101 Franklin in Tribeca into luxury condos. A conversion turns one use into another, here an older building into for-sale apartments. The Real Deal

Why it matters: Fresh conversion money shows lenders will still fund the right project, even while troubled offices change hands at a loss.

Distress Watch: Moinian Group modified the $131 million CMBS loan on 2 Washington Street in Battery Park City and signed a new tenant after the prior tenant, Sonder, went bankrupt. A CMBS loan is a mortgage bundled and sold to bond investors, so modifying it means the borrower and those investors agreed to new terms rather than foreclose. Commercial Observer

Sun Belt

SCALE Lending issued a $345 million construction loan for a multifamily project in Miami's Brickell neighborhood. A construction loan funds a building while it goes up and is paid back once the project is leased or sold. Yield PRO

Why it matters: A loan this big shows lenders still back new apartments in Brickell even as construction stalls nationally.

West Coast

Lincoln Property Company has doubled its Southern California portfolio over five years to about 78 million square feet. It now holds a $6 billion development pipeline across Los Angeles, Orange and San Diego counties. A pipeline is the set of projects a firm plans to build next. Commercial Observer

Why it matters: A pipeline that large is a bet that chronic housing shortages will keep Southern California space in demand despite costly financing.

International

ESR broke ground on a US$762 million logistics development in Greater Tokyo, one of its largest warehouse projects in the region. Logistics means warehouses and distribution centers that move goods. RE Talk Asia

Term of the Day

Defeasance

Swapping a loan's collateral for a basket of Treasury bonds so the owner can sell or refinance before the loan matures. The bonds generate the same payments the lender expected, so the lender is kept whole. Example: an owner with a $10 million loan buys Treasuries that cover the remaining payments, freeing the building to be sold.
