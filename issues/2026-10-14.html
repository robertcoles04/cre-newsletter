Top Story

Multifamily's slow rent recovery meets a refinancing wall

Apartment rents are climbing back only gradually, while loans taken out during the low-rate boom are now coming due. Owners who have to refinance face higher borrowing costs and rents that have not recovered enough to cover them.

Source: GlobeSt.com: Multifamily's slow rent recovery meets a refinancing wall

Why it matters A landlord who borrowed cheaply three years ago may owe more each month after refinancing than the building earns, which can force a sale or a fresh cash injection.

Top Stories

Unsolicited $2B bid for a 93-hotel portfolio draws doubts

An unsolicited $2 billion all-cash offer for a collection of 93 hotels has raised red flags.

Why it matters: sellers who get a surprise, possibly unfunded offer can waste months chasing it, while a credible bid would set a price marker for the whole hotel sector.

Source: The Real Deal: Unsolicited $2B bid for a 93-hotel portfolio draws doubts

Inflation accelerates to 3.7% as gas prices surge

Consumer prices rose 3.7% over the year in September, up from 3.4% in August, as gasoline costs jumped, the Bureau of Labor Statistics said. Prices climbed 0.6% for the month.

Why it matters: hotter inflation makes the Fed likelier to keep rates high, which keeps commercial real estate borrowing costs elevated and weighs on property values.

Source: Connect CRE: Inflation accelerates to 3.7% as gas prices surge

Wells Fargo exec: higher rates will hurt CRE values, 'it's just math'

A Wells Fargo executive warned that sustained higher interest rates will drag down commercial real estate valuations, calling the effect "just math." Higher rates raise the return buyers demand, which lowers what they are willing to pay.

Why it matters: owners counting on a quick rate cut to rescue values may be disappointed, and lenders could ask for more equity when loans come up for refinancing.

Source: The Business Journals: Wells Fargo exec: higher rates will hurt CRE values, 'it's just math'

Sun Belt multifamily recovers, but unevenly

Sun Belt apartment markets are returning to normal at very different speeds as oversupply from a decade-long building boom clears metro by metro. Some markets have absorbed their new units, while others still have too many to fill.

Why it matters: an investor cannot treat "the Sun Belt" as one bet, because a strong metro may be leasing up while a neighbor still cuts rents to fill empty units.

Source: Commercial Observer: Sun Belt multifamily recovers, but unevenly

Debt Markets

A lender has started foreclosure on a vacant $350 million office campus in West Los Angeles. The buildings sit empty, and the lender is moving to take control rather than keep waiting for the owner to land tenants or new financing. Westside Today Why it matters: when a lender forecloses on empty offices, it would rather own the building than hold a defaulted loan. Nearby owners also get a lower sale comp to contend with.

Trepp reported that the CMBS special servicing rate rose seven basis points in September to 11.49%, its highest level since February 2013. Special servicing means a loan has been handed to a workout specialist because the borrower is in trouble, so a rising rate points to more distress working through the system. Trepp Why it matters: bondholders who bought CMBS (bonds backed by pools of commercial mortgages) face bigger potential losses, and that makes new loans costlier to arrange.

Distress Watch: A North San Jose office campus has fallen into default on an $80 million loan, per The Real Deal. Default means the borrower has stopped making payments, which puts the lender in line to take the property.

Turning empty offices into apartments puts idle space back to work where demand for homes is strong. Term of the Day

SOFR

Short for the Secured Overnight Financing Rate, the benchmark most floating-rate commercial real estate loans are priced off. A lender quotes your rate as SOFR plus a margin: on a loan priced at SOFR plus two points, if SOFR were 4%, you would pay 6%. When SOFR rises, floating-rate borrowers owe more each month, so the choice between a fixed and floating loan comes down to where you think SOFR heads.
