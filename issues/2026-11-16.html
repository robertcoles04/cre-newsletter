Top Story

Existing-home sales fall to a 16-year low

A spike in mortgage rates pushed existing-home sales down to their slowest pace in 16 years, according to The Real Deal. Higher rates make monthly payments larger, so many would-be buyers simply wait.

Source: The Real Deal: Existing-home sales fall to a 16-year low

Why it matters Fewer home sales slow the whole housing chain, hurting agents, lenders and builders who depend on steady deal flow.

Top Stories

Analysts see near-term rate hikes, and a silver lining for CRE

GlobeSt reports growing signs that the Fed could raise rates again soon, which usually spells trouble for property values. The twist: some analysts argue the move could clear out weak owners and reset prices to levels that reward patient buyers.

Why it matters: Higher rates raise borrowing costs for everyone, but a reset in prices gives well-capitalized buyers a cheaper entry point.

Source: GlobeSt.com: Analysts see near-term rate hikes, and a silver lining for CRE

Deadly building collapse rattles New York City

New York City is reeling from a deadly building collapse, The Real Deal reports. The disaster puts a spotlight on how buildings are inspected and maintained.

Why it matters: City officials and owners may face tougher inspection rules, raising upkeep costs for buildings across the five boroughs.

Source: The Real Deal: Deadly building collapse rattles New York City

AT&T breaks ground on a $1.4B headquarters in Plano

AT&T started construction on a $1.4 billion headquarters in Plano, Texas, and expects to move staff in during late 2028, per The Real Deal. The project anchors a major corporate relocation to the Dallas area.

Why it matters: A large new headquarters pulls jobs and demand to Plano, lifting nearby office, retail and housing markets.

Source: The Real Deal: AT&T breaks ground on a $1.4B headquarters in Plano

Debt Markets

November brings $4.42 billion of commercial mortgage-backed securities (CMBS, bonds backed by pools of property loans) coming due, and $805 million of that has no clear way to refinance. That shortfall is the refi gap: the amount lenders may not get back when a loan matures. Yahoo Finance

Why it matters: Borrowers who cannot refinance may have to sell at a loss or hand the keys to their lender.

CBRE calls today's lending a "tale of two markets": money flows easily to strong assets like apartments and warehouses, while offices and weaker properties struggle to find any lender at all. MBA Newslink

Why it matters: Owners of older offices face a lending freeze, so many will need fresh equity or a sale to stay afloat.

Stonehenge landed a $233 million refinancing on a Midtown West apartment building in Manhattan after finishing a renovation. Lenders reward completed, income-producing projects. GlobeSt.com

Why it matters: A clean refi shows that strong rental buildings can still attract debt even while offices cannot.

A Berkeley waterfront hotel has defaulted on its loan and now faces possible foreclosure, meaning its lender could seize the property. Default happens when a borrower stops making required payments. The hotel's summary gives no dollar figures, only the warning that the lender may move to take it over. The Business Journals

Why it matters: A hotel owner missing loan payments is a warning sign to lenders, who may tighten terms on other hotel borrowers to protect themselves.

Distress Watch: A lender took ownership of a prominent apartment tower in West Raleigh after the owner defaulted on its loan, per The Business Journals.

Market Watch

Sun Belt

Dallas withdrew a $96 million incentive agreement for Hoque Global's $450 million Newpark district downtown after the developer missed its financing and building permit deadlines. The city pulled support once the project stalled. Bisnow

Why it matters: Losing a public incentive makes an already pricey project harder to fund, and other developers see that deadlines carry real consequences.

West Coast

Office development will not pencil out in most US markets for years, Mortgage Professional America reports. "Pencil out" means a project earns enough rent to cover its cost and turn a profit, and today's high building and borrowing costs make that math fail.

Why it matters: With no new offices breaking ground, existing buildings face less competition, but construction workers and lenders lose a steady source of deals. Mortgage Professional America

International

Great Portland Estates sold a London mixed-use block for £422.3 million, trimming its holdings in the city center. The sale shows buyers still want prime London property. Completely Retail News

Why it matters: A large sale at a firm price signals that global investors still trust London values, giving other owners there a comp to point to.

Quick Hits

  • The Broe Group entered Dallas with one of the nation's largest multifamily transactions of 2026, per Yield PRO. A new, deep-pocketed buyer entering a metro is a vote of confidence in that market's apartment demand.
  • Apartment concessions are climbing as owners defend a 94% occupancy rate, reports Yahoo Finance. A concession is a freebie like a month of no rent, so owners are trading income for full buildings.
  • At Austin's Mueller Flats, the borrower warned its servicer that rivals are offering months of free rent, per Commercial Observer. Heavy give-aways signal a glut of empty units competing for the same renters.
  • RXR bought a minority stake in Silverstein and MetroLoft's 55 Broad Street in Downtown Manhattan, reports Commercial Observer. Fresh equity from a new partner helps an owner pay down debt and keep a recapitalized building afloat.
  • The nine major apartment REITs sold far more property than they bought this year, according to Trepp. When public landlords become net sellers, they are raising cash and shrinking rather than expanding.
  • Service Properties Trust rejected a $2 billion cash bid, claiming its hotels could be worth double, per Bisnow. A rejected lowball offer shows management and bidders disagree sharply on what the assets are worth.

AI Infrastructure

Blackstone Digital Infrastructure Trust, a publicly traded REIT that buys and runs data centers, agreed to acquire a 75 percent interest in a $5 billion joint venture with Amazon Web Services. Data centers are the warehouse-like buildings full of servers that run cloud computing and artificial intelligence. Pairing a major landlord with Amazon locks in a large, creditworthy tenant and signals that investors still see data centers as one of the strongest corners of real estate. Commercial Observer

Term of the Day

Maturity wall

A cluster of loans all coming due around the same time, so borrowers must refinance or repay at once. Imagine $100 million of five-year loans made in one busy year; five years later they hit together. If rates are higher then, some owners cannot refinance and are forced to sell.
