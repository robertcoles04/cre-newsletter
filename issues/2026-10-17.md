## Market Spotlight

Offices are the week's clear stress point. Office loans bundled into CMBS reached a 12.2% delinquency rate, meaning about one in eight is behind on payments, topping the 2008 crisis peak. [Hokanews](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMif0FVX3lxTE83c0YxaXBOM2dBcWNFNUVsUlRRdXVGLTZHWWNjdnNmblF6MDJ4eEc2N0N0ZzRjOV9yU3QxRjYxeDlWOG9TcW9Eb0FaaWtMY1QxM1F1ZzFmTlJaMTVpWnNvVmRWeVlPVVN1eVFseGdid0h0cGN6UEpZeHBld3NaTWs?oc=5). High vacancy and expensive debt leave many towers worth less than their loans, so owners stop paying and hand back the keys. The picture is not all grim. In Chicago, the Zara founder paid $550 million for a trophy tower, one of the year's largest office trades. [Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/chicago/office/chicago-office-tower-sells-for-550m-to-zara-founder-amancio-ortega). "Trophy" means the best building in a market, and well-located, high-quality towers still attract deep-pocketed buyers, while older or half-empty ones struggle to refinance. Lenders watching these numbers will keep office loans scarce and pricey, squeezing weaker owners further.

## Term of the Day

**Preferred return:** the minimum yearly return investors must receive before the sponsor (the person running the deal, called the general partner) starts sharing in profits. Say a fund promises an 8% preferred return. Investors collect their 8% first, and only then does the sponsor take a cut. It aligns everyone, because the sponsor earns extra only after investors are paid.
