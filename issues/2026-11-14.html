Week in Review

The Trepp CMBS special servicing rate rose 25 basis points to 11.74% in October 2026, the highest since December 2012. Special servicing is when a loan moves to a workout specialist because the borrower is in trouble. Lodging drove the jump, making up 37% of the $3.01 billion that transferred that month. Trepp

SpaceX said it plans to spend $74B on data center campuses in Texas and Arizona, a major expansion of Elon Musk's firm. Bisnow

The Kraft Group, run by the New England Patriots owner, proposed a $16B, 10-building data center campus in Sutton, poised to be the region's largest. Bisnow

Market Spotlight

Data centers dominated the week. These are large buildings packed with computer servers that run AI and cloud services, and they need cheap land and heavy power. Developers pitched multibillion-dollar campuses across Texas, Arizona, and Massachusetts.

Who gains: landowners near power lines, construction firms, and local governments collecting new taxes. Who worries: neighbors facing noise and strain on the grid. The risk is concentration. Jensen Huang's exit from Nvidia shows how much the boom leans on a few chipmakers and tenants. Bisnow If AI demand cools, half-built campuses could sit empty.

AI in Real Estate Weekly

Property management software giants are racing to control AI agents, software assistants that can answer tenant messages, schedule repairs, and chase rent. Landlords and property managers use them to cut the hours staff spend on routine tasks. GlobeSt.com

Microsoft ended an older-generation GPU lease with Oracle, a sign the AI leasing market is splitting, with demand strong for new chips and weak for old ones. Data center owners holding dated hardware may struggle to re-lease it. BigGo Finance

Former Trump energy officials launched a Houston startup to mass produce modular data centers, prefabricated units meant to deploy faster than traditional builds. The Real Deal

Term of the Day

Balloon payment

A large lump sum of principal due when a loan matures. Say you borrow $10 million and only pay interest for 10 years. At the end, the full $10 million comes due at once. Borrowers usually refinance or sell to cover it, so a balloon is risky if rates have risen or lenders have pulled back.
