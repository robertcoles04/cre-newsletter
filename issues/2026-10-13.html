Top Story

Investors stop waiting on rate cuts

GlobeSt reports that commercial real estate buyers have stopped assuming lower rates will bail out deals, and now underwrite properties at current borrowing costs. That means lower prices offered, not optimistic ones. GlobeSt.com

Why it matters Sellers who still expect yesterday's prices will sit unsold, so the gap between buyer and seller keeps deal volume low.

Top Stories

BXP advances $2B Midtown office project

BXP's $2B office development in Midtown Manhattan has reached podium level, the base floors that carry the tower above, GlobeSt reports. The project is pressing ahead despite a soft office market. GlobeSt.com

Why it matters: A developer committing billions to new offices signals that top-tier Manhattan space still draws tenants, even as older buildings sit empty.

Microsoft sues JLL over NYC office injury

Microsoft has sued the brokerage JLL, asking it to cover any damages from a lawsuit by a construction worker hurt at its Fifth Avenue office project, Bisnow reports. The fight is over who is responsible for the forklift accident. Bisnow

Why it matters: Tenants and their vendors are pushing injury liability onto each other, a reminder that build-out contracts decide who pays when something goes wrong.

Lennar's Millrose accused of buying and renting homes at a loss

The Real Deal reports that Millrose, a company Lennar spun off to hold land, allegedly bought finished homes and rented them below cost instead. The claim questions how the vehicle was actually run. The Real Deal

Why it matters: How a land-banking spinoff uses its cash affects its investors, and sets a precedent other homebuilders copying the model will watch.

San Francisco keeps pushing office-to-housing

San Francisco is still trying to convert empty offices into homes, The Business Times reports, even though the math on such conversions is hard. The city sees housing as a way to refill a hollowed-out downtown. The Business Times

Why it matters: Conversions cut office supply and add apartments, which helps landlords of remaining offices and gives residents more places to live.

Debt Markets

Fewer commercial mortgage bonds are behind on payments, but GlobeSt reports a rise in loans that have just slipped into early delinquency. That early-stage trouble often turns into bigger problems months later. GlobeSt.com

Why it matters: Lenders watch early misses to guess future losses, so a rising count makes them stricter on new loans.

Apartment owners can find plenty of debt, but GlobeSt says equity, the owner's own cash in a deal, is scarce. Buyers struggle to raise the down-payment portion even when a bank will lend the rest. GlobeSt.com

Why it matters: Without new equity, multifamily sales stall, which slows price discovery and leaves sellers waiting.

GlobeSt looks at when it stops paying to keep extending a troubled office loan, the "extend and pretend" tactic lenders use to avoid taking a loss. At some point the math no longer works. GlobeSt.com

Why it matters: When extensions end, lenders foreclose or sell at a loss, which puts more discounted office buildings on the market.

Higher rates and rising costs are pushing more commercial real estate loans into distress, according to new reporting. Loans taken out in cheaper times now cost more to refinance, and climbing expenses leave owners with less cash to cover their payments. More borrowers are falling behind or asking lenders for relief. GlobeSt.com.

Why it matters: Distress means a loan is close to default, so more distress signals lenders could take losses and buyers could find discounted properties for sale.

Distress Watch: The loan tied to the former Seattle Times office has been sent to a special servicer, The Business Journals reports. A special servicer is the workout team that steps in when a borrower cannot pay as agreed. The Business Journals

Market Watch

Sun Belt

A partnership landed a $507M construction loan to build a residential tower in Miami Beach, REBusiness Online reports. Getting a large construction loan now, when lenders are cautious, shows confidence in South Florida apartment demand. REBusiness Online

Why it matters: New supply gives renters more options, but it also tests whether Miami rents can stay high enough to repay a large loan.

West Coast

An owner refinanced 1800 Avenue of the Stars in Century City with a $550M loan, Yahoo Finance reports. A refinancing replaces an old loan with a new one, often when the old one comes due. Yahoo Finance

Why it matters: A completed refi on prime LA office tells other owners that strong buildings can still find lenders, even in a weak office market.

International

WP Carey bought the British International School's South Loop campus for $71M, The Real Deal reports. The deal adds a leased school building to the REIT's portfolio of single-tenant properties. The Real Deal

Why it matters: Buyers like schools because a long lease to a stable tenant gives steady rent, which is easier to finance than risky space.

AI Infrastructure

TeraWulf is pivoting from bitcoin mining to leasing out AI data centers, signing $27B in long-term contracts, Pluang reports. Data centers are warehouses full of servers that run AI models, and they need huge amounts of power and space. Converting a mining operation into leased AI capacity gives landlords and power providers a hungry new tenant, while straining local electric grids that must supply it. Pluang

Term of the Day

GP/LP

Two roles in a real estate deal. The general partner (GP) finds, buys and runs the property, and puts in a small slice of the cash. Limited partners (LPs) are passive investors who supply most of the money and share the profits, but do not manage anything. For example, a GP might put in $1M and raise $9M from LPs on a $10M purchase.
