## Week in Review

Blackstone Digital Infrastructure Trust, a publicly traded landlord that buys and runs data centers, agreed to acquire a 75% interest in a joint venture with Amazon Web Services for $5 billion. The deal covers 13 powered shells, which are building shells wired for power but not yet fitted out, across two AWS campuses, and it is the trust's first investment [Commercial Observer](https://commercialobserver.com/2026/11/blackstone-digital-infrastructure-trust-aws-data-center-joint-venture/).

Service Properties Trust, a REIT that owns hotels and net-lease retail, passed on a $2 billion all-cash buyout offer, betting it can do better on its own [The Real Deal](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMingFBVV95cUxPY1VKdEZGRWZCR095VVlrQWVHMElyeUVfT1A5cW9VTjBfTzR0Rnd2b2hIaXY2N2FpNmVOLW8wTHNSQ09GTmotZV9TYTc0Z3AzZ3JfUjRRRHlyelh4ajZVLURtanY3ZEdKZDhPSW5zT1Zya2N4dkdsUkhLUlh5MWZQQjlrUkozc09VQXlIMWYtdE1YRl9nRjYzWVBlSlV0QQ?oc=5).

Office loans kept dragging on the CMBS market. Fitch Ratings said its overall US CMBS delinquency rate rose 14 basis points to 3.60% in October, and the office rate hit an all-time high of 9.33% [Connect CRE](https://www.connectcre.com/stories/office-cmbs-delinquencies-reach-all-time-high-in-october/).

## Market Spotlight

Data centers were the week's dominant property type, drawing the largest deals and the most capital. That rush came with a warning. The Fed raised concerns about whether AI returns will justify the money pouring into data centers, a caution that could cool spending [GlobeSt.com](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMikAFBVV95cUxPLW9Yb1dTR2pLcFRHckRFMEpNV2pWa2FmT3psVGUzSkhPTVBOTHRNcmg5Y19SdDk3emMzY3A3ZXlNYmd0Z1dQWHVFSG1oNFdSN1c0ZFFEbmZwRHExUkhyVHZwN3VlalRwbUQycVVCc015X2tubDFvQzhCclk2WEhnMHNHLWw1dVhTSElIWndUbUM?oc=5). Much of the buying targets powered shells, buildings wired for heavy electricity but not yet filled with servers, which are cheaper to buy and faster to finish. The risk is concentration. If AI demand slows, the newest capacity could sit empty, leaving owners and their lenders holding half-used buildings.

## Term of the Day

**Replacement cost:** what it would cost to build the same property from scratch today, including land, labor and materials. When a buyer pays below replacement cost, say $150 a square foot for a building that would cost $250 to rebuild, they get a discount no new competitor can match, because rivals must pay full freight to build.
