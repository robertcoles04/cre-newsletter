## Week in Review

Rexford Industrial sold $1.3 billion of Southern California industrial property in the third quarter, including a previously announced $1.2 billion portfolio, as tenant leasing rebounded. The move turns the region's biggest recent buyer into one of its largest sellers, a sign the REIT (a real estate investment trust, a company that owns income property and trades like a stock) thinks prices have recovered enough to cash out. [Commercial Observer](https://commercialobserver.com/2026/10/rexford-industrial-q3/)

Ken Griffin's firm, in a joint venture with Vornado Realty Trust and Rudin, landed $3.3 billion of construction financing to build a 66-story office tower at 350 Park Avenue in Manhattan. A construction loan that size for brand-new office space is rare right now and signals confidence in trophy Midtown buildings. [Commercial Observer](https://commercialobserver.com/2026/10/ken-griffin-citadel-350-park-manhattan/)

The founder of Zara, through his investment vehicle, bought Chicago's One North Wacker office tower for about $550 million, one of the city's largest deals of the year. A foreign buyer paying that much for a downtown Chicago office signals that overseas money sees value in discounted U.S. offices. ## Term of the Day

**Loss-to-lease:** the gap between what current tenants pay and what the same space would rent for today. If a tenant pays $20 per square foot on an old lease but the market is now $24, the owner has a $4 loss-to-lease, which is potential rent upside once that lease renews or resets.
