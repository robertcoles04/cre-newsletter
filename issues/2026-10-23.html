Griffin, Vornado and Rudin get the go-ahead at 350 Park Avenue

Ken Griffin, Vornado and Rudin are moving ahead with their plan for 350 Park Avenue, one of Manhattan's most closely watched development sites, The Real Deal reports. The green light clears a project that has been in the works for years. The Real Deal

Top Stories

Blackstone's Q3 profit fell as higher rates hit property

Blackstone's third-quarter distributable earnings dropped 9.2 percent from a year earlier to $1.72 billion, or $1.32 a share, though results still beat Wall Street forecasts. The firm blamed part of the decline on its commercial real estate arm facing higher capital costs. Commercial Observer

Why it matters: When pricier debt dents even Blackstone's earnings, smaller landlords and funds with less cushion are likely feeling sharper pain.

New York clears COPA, giving nonprofits first dibs on troubled buildings

New York's City Council passed COPA, which gives nonprofit groups the right of first refusal to buy more than 2,000 of the city's troubled apartment buildings, Bisnow reports. The bill now heads to Mayor Mamdani's desk. Bisnow

Why it matters: Private owners of distressed buildings may see their pool of buyers shrink, while tenants could gain a path to nonprofit ownership and steadier conditions.

Debt Markets

A Trepp analysis found $802 million of securitized debt tied to Chicago-area office buildings where a major tenant's lease expires well before the loan comes due. It counted 48 leases across 30 properties, with the typical lease ending roughly 20 months before loan maturity. Trepp

Why it matters: When a building's biggest tenant can leave before the loan matures, the owner may struggle to refinance, and the lender is left holding a weaker, emptier property as collateral.

Apartment REIT UDR set up a joint venture with Apollo that, according to GlobeSt, works more like a loan than an outright sale of its properties. The structure lets the landlord raise cash while keeping a stake. GlobeSt.com

Why it matters: Dressing financing up as a partnership lets an owner pull out cash without selling at today's low prices, a sign landlords would rather borrow than lock in a loss.

Market Watch

Sun Belt

Saks Global asked Dallas for $126 million in public support to keep its flagship Neiman Marcus store downtown, The Real Deal reports. The request shows a struggling luxury retailer leaning on a city to anchor a key property. The Real Deal

Why it matters: If Dallas pays to keep the store, it protects downtown foot traffic and nearby property values; if it refuses, a large retail space could go dark.

West Coast

Los Angeles County multifamily sales climbed 44 percent year over year to $3.3 billion, The Real Deal reports, a sign buyers are returning to apartment deals in the region. The Real Deal

Why it matters: More deals closing means buyers and sellers are agreeing on prices again, which gives apartment owners clearer values to point to when they refinance or sell.

International

Developer Tim Gurner unveiled a $1.75 billion plan to remake Geelong, Victoria's second city, pitching a Dubai-style waterfront precinct, the Australian Financial Review reports. AFR

Why it matters: A bet this size on a smaller city tests whether investors will fund ambitious projects outside the biggest metros while borrowing stays expensive.

Term of the Day

Mezzanine debt

A loan that sits between the senior mortgage and the owner's equity. It gets repaid after the main lender but before the owner, so it carries higher interest to match that extra risk. On a $10 million purchase with a $7 million mortgage, mezzanine debt might cover part of the remaining gap.
