## Market Spotlight

The apartment sector is this week's trouble spot. Yahoo Finance reports US apartment landlords face distress not seen since 2008, with a $2 trillion debt bill coming due. [Yahoo Finance](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMimgFBVV95cUxNX2dqSWpYRnB1eGN2N2MyUU41OXBLVkt4QnlWa28xRzMzREMxU3BqN0x4emRHYUVBc2JRLThwVXBQazJtclNLNEV5X0ZZUHhHTG9JREJFTDRiQTY5TmlTYTJiN2FETm1RZzN5aW5Wa0ZGMlpydHFFRVhOZDlnQjM1R0dKeE5lWjdFa28ybWxHck54YXFrSGY2T1lR?oc=5)

Here is the mechanism. Many apartment owners took out short-term loans when rates were low, planning to refinance before the loans matured. That bill is now due, and new loans cost far more. Owners who cannot cover the higher payments may have to sell at a loss or hand buildings back to lenders. Renters could feel it too, since strained owners often cut maintenance. A wave of forced sales would also reset values lower, giving well-capitalized buyers a chance to pick up buildings cheaply.

## Term of the Day

**Construction loan:** short-term financing for a building project, paid out in stages as the work gets done rather than all at once. For example, a developer might draw $2 million after the foundation is poured and more once the framing is up. The lender releases cash as milestones are hit, which limits its risk if the project stalls.
