Top Story

Apollo and UDR launch a $2 billion apartment venture

UDR and Apollo will form a long-term joint venture built around a 19-property, 7,059-unit apartment portfolio valued at about $2.0 billion. Apollo is investing roughly $1.34 billion of equity for a two-thirds interest. Connect CRE

Why it matters A joint venture lets UDR cash out part of its portfolio while keeping a hand in management, and it signals big investors still want apartments despite high rates.

Top Stories

CBRE buys data center supplier Onnec for $610 million

CBRE acquired Onnec Group, a London-based data center equipment provider, in an all-cash deal, with up to $400 million more to come based on Onnec's growth. The buy extends CBRE's push into the physical plumbing of data centers. Commercial Observer

Why it matters: a services giant buying the gear supplier shows firms want to own more of the AI buildout, beyond brokering the leases around it.

Immigration raids are draining construction crews

Builders say immigration raids have thinned their workforces, with one firm reporting that its crews are gone. Fewer workers means slower jobsites. The Real Deal

Why it matters: labor shortages push up building costs and delay delivery, which squeezes developer budgets and can slow new supply in tight markets.

Manhattan office leasing is strong, but landlords still sweeten deals

Even with Manhattan office leasing running hot, the concessions landlords offer are at an all-time high. Deal sweeteners include months of free rent and money to build out a tenant's space. The Real Deal

Why it matters: headline rents can look healthy while landlords quietly give value away, so a tenant's real cost may be far below the sticker number.

The Plaza Hotel will close for four years

New York's landmark Plaza Hotel is set to close for about four years. A shutdown that long pulls a famous property off the market for an extended stretch. The Real Deal

Why it matters: a multi-year closure means lost room revenue now, a bet the owner expects to pay off once the hotel reopens.

Debt Markets

A $1.3 billion Blackstone commercial mortgage-backed securities (CMBS) loan, now carrying a $517.7 million balance, entered special servicing after missing its October 2026 maturity. Special servicing means a troubled loan gets handed to a specialist who negotiates a workout or forces a sale. Commercial Observer

Why it matters: even the biggest owners are caught by Sun Belt apartment stress, so lenders there are likely to drive harder bargains on refinancings.

Northwind Group closed its third real estate credit fund with $1 billion in commitments, double its original $500 million goal, and has already originated $1.5 billion in loans. The fund is a pool of private money that lends directly to property owners. Commercial Observer

Why it matters: private lenders are stepping into gaps that cautious banks left, giving borrowers more places to find money, often at a higher price.

Webstar admitted a nearly $19 million accounting error on its $3.8 billion Forge Atlanta project and fell further behind on its loan. The error raises doubts about its ability to raise the funds the development needs. Bisnow

Why it matters: a bookkeeping mistake can rattle lenders and freeze a megaproject, leaving a city with a stalled site instead of new buildings.

Distress Watch: a Loop office-to-residential conversion at 330 S. Wells St. is set for a foreclosure auction next month, after a judge ruled its $28.2 million mortgage was in default. Bisnow

Market Watch

Sun Belt

An Orlando developer walked away from a $200 million buyout in West Palm Beach, ending a planned purchase. Backing out of a deal this size points to a buyer rethinking price or financing. The Real Deal

Why it matters: when buyers abandon large deals, it signals sellers may need to cut asking prices for transactions to close.

West Coast

A debt deal on a downtown Los Angeles office tower is being read as a turning point for a battered office market. The transaction centers on the loan behind a troubled building. grihik.com

Why it matters: fresh capital moving into downtown LA office debt hints that some investors think prices there have fallen far enough to buy.

International

Canada's count of approved-but-unstarted homes is climbing even as housing starts slide. Projects are getting the green light but not breaking ground. Storeys

Why it matters: approvals that do not turn into construction mean higher costs or financing trouble are holding builders back, which keeps housing supply tight.

AI Infrastructure

U.S. industrial leasing hit a four-year high as demand for AI infrastructure, the warehouses, data centers and power-hungry buildings that run AI, picked up speed. Tenants tied to that buildout are absorbing large blocks of space. Institutional Real Estate, Inc. Warehouse owners and industrial landlords gain from the extra demand, while tenants face tighter space and firmer rents.

Term of the Day

1031 exchange

A tax rule that lets a property owner defer capital gains taxes by rolling the sale proceeds into a similar property. Sell a $3 million building for a $1 million gain, reinvest in another, and you push the tax bill down the road. The seller gains flexibility; the government waits for its money.
