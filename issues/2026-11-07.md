## Market Spotlight

Data centers ran through the week's biggest stories, and the theme was supply, not demand. Data centers are the warehouses full of servers that run cloud computing and AI, and they need enormous amounts of power, water and land. Texas holds roughly a quarter of planned U.S. capacity. When its voters elected a governor who promised to pause approvals, the question flipped to who actually gets to build, reports [Commercial Observer](https://commercialobserver.com/2026/11/data-centers-blue-wave-resources/). That backlash showed up nationally too, as Tuesday's results put local power and land fights front and center, per [Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/economy/the-results-are-in-what-we-know-so-far-about-cres-midterms-shake-out). Developers and the utilities serving them lose when permits stall. Communities worried about strained grids and water gain leverage. For investors, a project that cannot secure power or approval is a stranded bet, which is why a political shift can reprice this corner of real estate fast.

## Term of the Day

**IRR (internal rate of return):** the yearly return on an investment that accounts for when each dollar comes in or goes out. Getting cash back sooner raises it. If you put in 100 and collect 110 a year later, your IRR is about 10%. Investors use it to compare deals with different timelines.
