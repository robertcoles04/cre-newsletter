Week in Review

A Manhattan Supreme Court judge sided with Mayor Zohran Mamdani on his rent freeze, rejecting a challenge that landlords brought against the city's Rent Guidelines Board. Commercial Observer

Brookfield announced a 10 percent stake in the 13-property Hudson Square portfolio, a $3.5B set of Lower Manhattan offices, stepping in where Hines had been. Commercial Observer The same deal was reported by GlobeSt.com.

TSMC is eyeing a 3,000 acre site near Aggieland in Texas for a $22.6B chip plant. The Real Deal

Vornado sold a 49% stake in a Grand Central office tower at a $723M valuation, putting a fresh price tag on Midtown space. GlobeSt.com

Market Spotlight

New York ran through almost every major story this week. On the policy side, Mayor Mamdani won a court fight to freeze regulated rents. Commercial Observer That caps what owners of rent-stabilized buildings can charge and squeezes their income, while shielding tenants from increases. On the investment side, buyers kept circling Manhattan offices, with Brookfield buying into the Hudson Square portfolio and Vornado selling a stake in a Grand Central tower. Commercial Observer GlobeSt.com The split is the story: rent policy is tightening returns on apartments, while patient capital still sees a floor under office values.

AI in Real Estate Weekly

AI companies are racing for space in New York, a city that does not have much to spare. The New York Times Their growth pushes up demand for the scarce space that fits their needs.

Data center leasing, the warehouses full of servers that train and run AI, hit 50 million square feet, showing how much physical space the AI boom now needs. Wisevoter

Digital Realty reported record data center leasing and raised its guidance, a sign landlords serving AI tenants expect demand to keep climbing. Kalkine Media

Term of the Day

Cash sweep

When a borrower breaks a loan rule, the lender can grab the property's leftover cash after bills and use it to pay down the debt. Say a building throws off $100,000 a month in extra cash; a sweep sends that to the lender instead of the owner, until the loan is back in good standing.
