Top Stories

Macerich pays $250M for San Jose's Oakridge Mall

Mall owner Macerich acquired the Oakridge mall in San Jose for $250M, growing its Bay Area retail footprint. The company expects the center to lift occupancy and income. Bisnow

Why it matters: A REIT paying up for a strong mall, while weaker retail drives loan defaults, shows buyers are splitting good centers from troubled ones.

Amancio Ortega pushes Chicago bets to $1B

Zara founder Amancio Ortega bought a Gold Coast apartment building in Chicago, lifting his local investments to about $1B. The purchase adds to a steady run of U.S. buys by his investment arm. The Real Deal

Why it matters: A deep-pocketed foreign buyer concentrating on Chicago apartments gives local owners a comp to cite when they refinance or sell.

SF's latest AI lease signals a tech return

A wave of AI firms is signing new leases in San Francisco, bringing tenants back to the city once hollowed out by remote work. The latest deal adds to a run of AI-driven office demand. The Real Deal

Why it matters: New AI leases absorb empty office space, which helps San Francisco landlords refill towers and slows the slide in downtown values.

Congress questions Brookfield's manufactured housing deal

Lawmakers are scrutinizing Brookfield's acquisition of manufactured housing, often called mobile home parks. The review could shape how big owners run these communities. The Real Deal

Why it matters: Federal pressure on a large owner could cap rent growth, trimming the returns investors expected from these parks.

Debt Markets

Ashcroft Capital may lose nearly 1,700 Sun Belt apartments after allegedly defaulting on a $365.6M multifamily loan. The syndicator pooled money from many small investors to buy apartments, then borrowed heavily against them. Bisnow

Why it matters: When a syndicator defaults, the lender takes the buildings and the small investors can lose their stake, a warning for anyone buying into these deals.

Large investors are increasingly walking away from apartment loans they can no longer carry, leaving lenders to absorb the losses, the WSJ reports. WSJ

Why it matters: If big borrowers default without penalty, lenders tighten terms for everyone, making new apartment loans harder to get.

The Chetrit Group's 26 Broadway office tower, the historic Standard Oil Building, had its appraised value cut to $147M as the owner faces mounting financial and legal trouble. Bisnow

Why it matters: A lower appraisal means the loan may now exceed the building's worth, so the lender could demand repayment or take the tower.

Distress Watch: Mount Point bought a distressed Rosemont office tower outside Chicago at roughly half its former value, per The Real Deal. The Real Deal

Term of the Day

Cap rate compression

When cap rates fall, meaning buyers accept a lower yearly return relative to price. Because the rate is income divided by price, a lower rate pushes values up for the same income. If a building earns $1M a year and the market cap rate drops from 8% to 6%, its value rises from about $12.5M to roughly $16.7M. Owners gain; new buyers earn less.
