Top Stories

Dallas industrial portfolio trades for $312 million

A private REIT bought nine shallow-bay warehouses across north Dallas from a regional developer. The buildings are 96% leased with an average remaining term of four years.

Why it matters: Small-bay industrial is still drawing institutional bids, which supports pricing for owners of similar product in secondary Texas markets.

Bisnow

Lender takes back Midtown office building

A debt fund completed a deed-in-lieu on a 22-story Midtown Manhattan office building after occupancy fell to 61%. The loan had been extended twice since 2023.

Why it matters: Lenders choosing ownership over another extension signals they expect to recover more by repositioning than by waiting for a refinance.

Commercial Observer

Phoenix apartment starts fall to a five-year low

Permits for buildings with five or more units dropped 38% from a year earlier in the Phoenix metro. Developers cited construction loan costs and slower rent growth.

Why it matters: Fewer starts now means less new supply in 2028, which helps landlords who can hold through the current lease-up wave.

Term of the Day

Deed-in-lieu: A borrower hands the property to the lender instead of going through foreclosure. In the Midtown story above, the lender took the keys directly, which is usually faster and cheaper than a court process for both sides.
